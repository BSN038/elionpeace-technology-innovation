THE BUSINESS PROBLEM

Introduction

Examining recruitment, retention, workforce suitability and regulatory compliance within the UK private security industry.

The UK private security industry operates within a regulatory framework overseen by the Security Industry Authority (SIA). Individuals carrying out licensable security activities must hold the appropriate licence, depending on their role and employment arrangements (Security Industry Authority, 2026). Organisations deploying security personnel must therefore consider licensing requirements when selecting workers for specific assignments. This responsibility extends beyond confirming that a licence exists, as the licence must also permit the activities involved in the assignment (Security Industry Authority, 2026).

Recruitment, retention and workforce suitability remain important challenges within the UK private security industry. Research conducted by the Security Industry Authority (2022) identified difficulties experienced by some security companies in recruiting and retaining personnel, including barriers associated with training requirements, irregular working hours and limited flexibility. Similarly, Mitie (2024) reported that 33% of surveyed security decision-makers considered the limited pool of applicants a challenge when recruiting qualified professionals.

Beyond recruitment, concerns about training standards have also been documented. The SIA reported a 50% increase in training malpractice cases managed during 2024–25 (Security Industry Authority, 2025). Although these findings do not establish widespread professional incompetence, they highlight the importance of recruitment, training quality and workforce suitability. For ElionPeace, the business problem is therefore not simply finding available security personnel, but identifying appropriately licensed professionals whose verified competencies, experience and availability match the requirements of specific assignments.

The Proposed Innovation

To address these challenges, this project proposes an AI-assisted workforce matching system for ElionPeace, a developing digital platform designed to connect security professionals with organisations requiring security services. The proposed approach would combine mandatory licence checks with an assessment of verified qualifications, relevant experience, availability and job-specific competencies.

Artificial Intelligence would support the identification and recommendation of suitable candidates, while final assignment decisions would remain subject to human review. The use of AI in recruitment and candidate selection requires careful consideration of potential discrimination, transparency and data protection risks (Department for Science, Innovation and Technology, 2024). The project therefore considers AI as a decision-support tool rather than a replacement for professional judgement.

Research Aim and Scope

The aim of this project is to critically evaluate how AI-assisted workforce matching could support more effective staffing decisions within the UK private security industry. Using ElionPeace as the proposed business application, the research examines whether combining regulatory eligibility checks with competency-based recommendations could improve candidate suitability and reduce the administrative effort involved in matching workers to assignments.

The research also considers practical limitations, including data quality, algorithmic bias, transparency and human oversight. Its scope is limited to the proposed matching and selection process; it does not involve developing a fully operational AI model or replacing existing SIA licensing and training requirements. The findings inform a proposed digital solution and a customised Digital Maturity Model for ElionPeace.

CRITICAL TECHNOLOGY ANALYSIS

Innovative Technology

Evaluating how rule-based eligibility checks, AI-assisted recommendations and human oversight could support more effective workforce matching.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) can support workforce matching by analysing candidate information against the requirements of available assignments. Ricci et al. (2022) explain how recommender systems use information to identify and suggest relevant options, providing a technological foundation for matching workers with suitable opportunities. Unlike systems that rely exclusively on predefined rules, machine-learning models can identify patterns in data to inform recommendations.

However, algorithmic decision-making in recruitment is not necessarily objective or fair. Köchling and Wehner (2020), in a systematic review of 36 studies, identified risks of discrimination and unequal treatment associated with these technologies. For ElionPeace, AI-assisted matching is therefore proposed as a means of supporting competency-based recommendations, rather than automatically determining which security professional should receive an assignment.

The Proposed Hybrid Approach

The proposed ElionPeace system would adopt a hybrid approach, combining rule-based eligibility checks with AI-assisted candidate recommendations. Initially, predefined rules would assess whether a security professional holds the appropriate SIA licence and meets essential assignment requirements, including availability.

Eligible candidates would then be compared against job-specific criteria, such as verified training, relevant experience and professional competencies. Çelik Ertuğrul and Bitirim (2025) identify content-based, knowledge-based and hybrid approaches among the methods used in job recommender systems, providing a basis for this proposed design.

A future machine-learning component could analyse relevant candidate and assignment data to generate suitability recommendations, although its performance would require testing before implementation. Recruiters would review the recommendations and supporting information before making final assignment decisions, retaining human responsibility for the selection process.

Comparing Workforce Matching Approaches

Traditional workforce matching relies heavily on manual assessment, whereas rule-based systems can filter candidates against predefined requirements. AI-assisted approaches may offer additional capabilities when evaluating complex candidate information, although their effectiveness must be assessed rather than assumed.

Will et al. (2023), in a systematic review of 22 studies, found evidence of advantages in AI-supported recruitment efficiency and performance, while also identifying more negative perceptions of AI among candidates and recruiters. However, these findings cannot be directly generalised to security workforce assignments.

For ElionPeace, a hybrid approach would retain transparent SIA eligibility checks while evaluating AI-generated recommendations against simpler matching methods. This comparison would help determine whether the additional technological complexity provides measurable operational value.

Risks, Limitations and Responsible AI

Despite its potential, AI-assisted matching presents challenges relating to data quality, fairness and accountability. Köchling and Wehner (2020) demonstrate that algorithmic recruitment decisions can produce discriminatory outcomes, while the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (2024) emphasises the importance of transparency, risk assessment and appropriate safeguards.

For ElionPeace, inaccurate competency records or biased historical data could undermine candidate recommendations. The proposed system would therefore require reliable verification processes, regular performance and fairness testing, clear explanations of recommendations and meaningful human oversight before deployment.

HOW THE PROPOSED SOLUTION WORKS

Proposed Solution

Exploring how ElionPeace could connect security organisations with appropriately licensed and competent professionals through a structured, AI-assisted workforce matching process.

The proposed ElionPeace solution would provide a digital platform through which UK security organisations could advertise assignments and identify suitable security professionals. Employers would specify the location, shift times, required SIA licence and job-specific competencies, while workers would maintain profiles containing their qualifications, relevant experience and availability.

Before candidates were recommended, the proposed platform would assess mandatory eligibility requirements, including the appropriate SIA licence for the assignment. Licence validity would need to be confirmed through an authorised verification process before any assignment was approved.

Eligible professionals could then be assessed through an AI-assisted matching process, with recommendations presented to employers for review. This approach would support more structured selection decisions without removing human responsibility for assigning personnel.

Employer and Security Professional Experience

From the employer's perspective, the proposed user journey would involve accessing a shortlist of eligible security professionals through the ElionPeace platform. Each candidate's profile would display relevant qualifications, verified competencies, professional experience and availability, allowing employers to compare suitability against the assignment requirements.

Employers could review the information supporting each recommendation before confirming their preferred candidate. Security professionals would receive assignment details and could accept or decline the opportunity, subject to the platform's proposed confirmation process.

The completed assignment would then be recorded to support future monitoring and evaluation. These interactions would require usability testing to establish whether the proposed workflow is clear, practical and accessible to both user groups.

Potential Benefits and Implementation Challenges

The proposed solution could reduce repetitive administrative screening and help employers compare candidates against consistent, job-related criteria. However, these benefits would depend on accurate records, reliable licence verification and clearly defined competency requirements.

Implementation would also involve development costs, secure data management and training for employers using the platform. Before introducing AI-assisted recommendations, ElionPeace would need to compare their accuracy and usefulness with a simpler rule-based approach.

Potential evaluation measures could include time taken to shortlist candidates, the proportion meeting mandatory requirements and employer assessments of candidate suitability. These measures would help establish whether the additional technological complexity delivers meaningful operational value.

PLANNING DIGITAL TRANSFORMATION

Digital Maturity Model

A five-level model showing how ElionPeace could move from manual workforce coordination towards responsible AI-assisted matching and continuous improvement.

A customised Digital Maturity Model is proposed to evaluate how ElionPeace could improve security workforce recruitment and assignment practices. Van Tonder et al. (2024) identify technology, organisational processes, people and strategy as important dimensions of digital maturity. This shows that digital transformation involves more than introducing new software.

Based on these principles, the proposed ElionPeace model has five progressive levels, starting with manual recruitment and moving towards AI-assisted matching and continuous evaluation. Each level builds on the previous one. Progress would depend on reliable information, regulatory compliance, organisational readiness and measurable improvements in workforce selection.

These levels represent a proposed framework for future development. They are not an assessment of ElionPeace's current digital maturity. The purpose is to identify what the organisation would need to establish before moving towards more advanced technology.

LEVEL 03 / 05

Rule-Based Matching

At this level, transparent rules would check mandatory requirements, including the appropriate SIA licence, availability and assignment criteria. This would provide a structured foundation before introducing AI-assisted recommendations.

Moving Between Maturity Levels

The model begins at Level 1, where recruitment and assignment coordination depend mainly on telephone calls, emails and manual records. Level 2 introduces structured worker profiles, assignment information and documented licence verification. These records would need to be accurate, regularly updated and securely managed.

At Level 3, rule-based matching would filter candidates according to mandatory licensing, availability and job requirements. Level 4 would introduce AI-assisted recommendations using verified competencies and relevant experience, while keeping the final decision with an authorised person.

Level 5 would focus on continuous evaluation. ElionPeace could review matching accuracy, recruitment efficiency, fairness and feedback from employers. The findings could then inform improvements to the system.

Moving to a higher level should depend on evidence that the previous stage works reliably. For example, introducing AI before worker records and licence checks are dependable could create more problems rather than improve recruitment. This is why the model places data quality and compliance before more advanced matching technology.

Measuring Progress and Readiness

To assess progress, ElionPeace could use practical measures such as licence verification accuracy, completeness of competency records, time required to shortlist candidates and employer satisfaction with recommended workers. These measures would help determine whether each stage delivers improvements in the selection process.

Implementation would also require investment, secure data management, staff training and reliable verification procedures. Technology alone would not solve recruitment problems if the information used by the system were inaccurate or the people using it were not properly prepared.

For this reason, the proposed model prioritises regulatory compliance, reliable data and demonstrated operational value over technological complexity. It provides a structured way for ElionPeace to develop its digital capabilities while maintaining responsibility for workforce selection decisions.

CRITICAL EVALUATION

Conclusion and Recommendations

A critical reflection on the potential value, practical limitations and priorities for developing the proposed ElionPeace solution.

This project has examined how AI-assisted workforce matching could help address recruitment and assignment challenges within the UK private security industry. From my analysis, I consider the main problem to be more than finding available security workers. The real challenge is identifying professionals who meet the legal requirements and have the right competencies, experience and availability for a particular assignment. Holding an SIA licence is essential, but it does not automatically make someone suitable for every security role.

I believe the proposed hybrid approach offers a reasonable way to address this problem. Rule-based checks would establish whether candidates meet mandatory requirements, while AI could help employers compare eligible professionals against more detailed job criteria. However, my research has also made me question whether using AI would always produce better results. A more advanced system is not necessarily a more effective one, particularly if the information used to generate recommendations is incomplete or unreliable.

One important limitation is that the proposed matching system has not been tested with real recruitment data. Therefore, I cannot conclude that it would reduce recruitment time, improve candidate suitability or produce fairer outcomes. These remain potential benefits that would need to be demonstrated through testing. I also recognise that introducing AI could create additional costs and risks, particularly in relation to data protection, algorithmic bias and the transparency of recommendations.

Developing the Digital Maturity Model has also influenced my view of how ElionPeace should approach innovation. Rather than starting with the most advanced technology, I consider it more practical to build reliable digital records and a transparent rule-based selection process first. This would provide a stronger foundation for assessing whether AI could offer additional value at a later stage.

My Recommendations

Based on the research and limitations identified, I would recommend the following priorities before developing an operational matching platform.

01

Start with Reliable Verification

I would prioritise accurate worker records, appropriate SIA licence verification and clearly defined competency requirements. Without reliable information, neither rule-based matching nor AI recommendations can be trusted.

02

Test Before Introducing AI

I would first develop and evaluate a simpler rule-based matching process. AI-assisted recommendations should only be introduced if testing shows a meaningful improvement in efficiency or candidate suitability.

03

Keep People Responsible

I would retain human review for final assignment decisions and introduce regular checks for fairness, accuracy and data protection. Employers should be able to understand the reasons behind recommendations.

Final Reflection

Overall, I see potential in using AI to support security workforce matching, but I would not recommend introducing it simply because the technology is available. Its value should be judged by whether it helps employers make better-informed decisions and improves the selection process without compromising regulatory compliance or fairness.

For me, the most important outcome of this project is recognising that successful innovation depends on understanding the business problem before choosing the technology. In the case of ElionPeace, the priority should be to establish a reliable, transparent and practical matching process. AI could then become a useful part of that process, provided its benefits can be demonstrated and its risks properly managed.

FINAL TAKEAWAY

The value of ElionPeace would not come from using AI alone, but from combining regulatory compliance, verified professional competencies and responsible technology to support better workforce selection decisions.
